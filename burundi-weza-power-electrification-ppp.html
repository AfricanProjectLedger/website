Burundi signs $1.4bn electricity-distribution PPP with Weza Power

A 25-year concession signed on 15 October is designed to mobilise US$1.4 billion for electricity distribution infrastructure, extend service across roughly half of Burundi and provide first-time or improved access for an estimated eight million people.

Executive brief

The Government of Burundi and Weza Power, a company developed by Anzana Electric Group, signed the public-private partnership on 15 October 2026. Burundi’s Ministry of Mineral Resources, Energy, Industry, Trade and Tourism confirmed the contract on 18 October. The private concessionaire will invest in, expand and operate the distribution network in its designated area; state utility REGIDESO will continue to supply electricity through the national transmission network and retain distribution responsibilities in the other half of the country.

The project partners say the blended-finance structure is intended to mobilise commercial and concessional capital, with participation from development-finance and bilateral partners including the World Bank Group, IFC, MIGA, African Development Bank, U.S. International Development Finance Corporation and Gridworks. The announced programme includes 52,000 kilometres of new or upgraded distribution lines and more than 1.2 million new household and business connections.

Why it matters

This is a material shift from electrification targets and project preparation to a signed, long-term national-scale distribution concession. The model places investment and operating responsibilities with a private concessionaire while retaining a role for the state utility and development-finance partners. The partners target raising electricity access from around 15% to more than 50% by 2030, with an estimated eight million people receiving first-time or improved access.

For the market, the significance is not only the headline capital mobilisation. A distribution programme at this scale could activate sustained demand for line construction, transformers, metering, connections, network operations and maintenance. The US$1.4 billion is a programme mobilisation target, not evidence that the entire amount has already been disbursed.

What to watch

  • Publication of the concession’s detailed financing plan, capital deployment schedule and allocation of risks.
  • Initial procurement packages for distribution lines, transformers, metering and connection works.
  • Tariff and service-performance safeguards, including reliability and affordability for newly connected customers.
  • Verified progress against connection, network-extension and electrification targets through 2030.
