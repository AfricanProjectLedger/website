Cameroon authorises €347.5m World Bank loan for Douala–Bangui corridor

A presidential decree authorises signature of the IBRD credit agreement for Cameroon’s portion of a regional corridor programme connecting Douala with Bangui.

Executive brief

Cameroon issued Decree No. 2026/321 on 2 October 2026 authorising the government to sign a €347.5 million credit agreement with the World Bank’s International Bank for Reconstruction and Development for the Douala–Bangui Economic Corridor Project.

The wider World Bank programme was approved in June with a US$1.12 billion multiphase envelope. Its first phase directs US$407 million from IBRD and US$18 million from IDA to Cameroon, alongside US$90 million for the Central African Republic and US$10 million for CEMAC. Planned interventions include climate-resilient road rehabilitation, logistics hubs, feeder roads, axle-load controls and trade-facilitation reforms.

Why this is APL-worthy

The corridor extends for more than 1,400 kilometres and carries over 80% of the Central African Republic’s external trade. Cameroon’s sovereign authorisation is a material step toward activating the largest national financing component of a cross-border transport and logistics programme.

What to watch

Formal execution of the loan agreement, effectiveness and disbursement, procurement packages, contractor mobilisation and measurable reductions in journey times and border friction will determine the programme’s delivery impact.
