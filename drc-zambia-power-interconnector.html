Partners advance $300m DRC–Zambia power interconnector

FIS-RDC, EnPower, Gridworks and Trafigura have signed a strategic framework agreement to finance, build and operate a 200-kilometre cross-border transmission line into the DRC Copperbelt.

Executive brief

The Kalumbila–Kolwezi Interconnector Project is designed as a 700 MW high-voltage link between Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It would enable up to 550 MW of competitively priced electricity to flow into the DRC’s mining heartland, with provision for future capacity above 1 GW.

The project is estimated to cost $300 million. Gridworks, which is owned and funded by British International Investment, intends to become the lead equity investor and majority shareholder. The DRC’s strategic investment fund, FIS-RDC, and project developer EnPower also intend to invest equity, while Trafigura has agreed to arrange a significant share of the debt financing. Discussions are under way with Zambian entities on possible co-investment.

The partners say the project has reached the final development stage and that concessions, licences and authorisations are secured. The agreement is a material development milestone, but it is not financial close; final capital commitments and construction mobilisation remain to be confirmed.

Why this is APL-worthy

KKIP is positioned as Africa’s first privately developed and financed cross-border transmission project of its kind. It links institutional equity, sovereign strategic capital and commercial debt around infrastructure intended to relieve a power deficit exceeding 1 GW and unlock mining and refining capacity in the Copperbelt.

What to watch

Watch for binding equity commitments, the debt package arranged by Trafigura, any Zambian institutional participation, financial close, EPC appointment and a confirmed construction timetable. Offtake terms and access rules will determine how much capacity serves mining demand versus wider consumers.
