Scatec and EBRD establish Egypt platform targeting 3.6 GW of renewables

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD) and Scatec have established an investment platform for renewable-energy projects in Egypt. EBRD has committed up to US$221 million, while the initial portfolio targets 2.7 GW of solar, 900 MW of wind and 4.2 GWh of battery energy storage.

Executive brief

Scatec and EBRD announced the platform on 22 October 2026. The vehicle, Egypt Renewables Holdings B.V., is structured with Scatec holding 60% and EBRD 40%. EBRD's commitment of up to US$221 million comprises approximately US$170 million in equity, with the balance provided through project-level guarantees.

The platform is designed to finance the acquisition, development and construction of renewable-energy assets. Its initial pipeline is expected to include interests in the Obelisk solar-and-storage project, the Shadwan wind project, the first phase of the Egypt Aluminium solar project (Dandara), and Energy Valley. These are portfolio targets and ownership interests—not a claim that every project has reached financial close or construction.

Capital and capacity

  • EBRD commitment: up to US$221 million, including approximately US$170 million of equity and project-level guarantees.
  • Platform ownership: Scatec 60%; EBRD 40%.
  • Target solar capacity: 2.7 GW.
  • Target wind capacity: 900 MW.
  • Target battery storage: 4.2 GWh.
  • Expected portfolio output: approximately 11 TWh of electricity annually once commissioned, with potential emissions reductions of up to 5 million tonnes of CO₂ per year, according to EBRD.

Why it matters

This is more than a single-project loan. It creates an institutional-capital platform intended to finance a pipeline of utility-scale generation and storage assets, sharing ownership and project risk between a specialist developer and a multilateral lender. The combination of solar, wind and multi-hour storage is relevant to Egypt's energy security and the integration of variable renewable generation.

For APL readers, the important distinction is between the platform commitment and project-level execution. The US$221 million is EBRD's commitment to the vehicle, not the total capital cost of the entire 3.6 GW generation portfolio. The next test is how quickly the platform converts its initial project interests into final investment decisions, effective financing, construction milestones and grid-connected capacity.

Next proof points

  • Completion of the platform's ownership and investment arrangements.
  • Project-by-project disclosure of financing, equity contributions and any remaining conditions.
  • Construction and delivery milestones for Shadwan, Energy Valley and the relevant solar projects.
  • Grid connection, storage commissioning and verified operating output as assets enter service.
