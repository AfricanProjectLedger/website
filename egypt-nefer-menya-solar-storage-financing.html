Nefer Minya secures up to $566m for 1.2 GW solar-plus-storage project

HAU Energy and Infinity Power have announced a debt financing package of up to US$566 million for the Nefer Minya project in Egypt. The development combines a 1.2 GW solar photovoltaic plant with 600 MWh of battery storage and is expected to become Africa's largest solar PV plant.

Executive brief

The financing package includes up to US$265 million arranged by the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD). The EBRD facility comprises US$160 million from the Bank's own resources, a US$75 million B-loan mobilised from Bank of Jordan, and up to US$30 million in concessional financing from the Clean Technology Fund. Development finance institutions DEG, FMO and Proparco are also participating in the wider debt package.

The project company, Nefer Menya for Renewable Energy S.A.E., is owned by Infinity Power and HAU Energy. The plant is planned for Minya governorate and will combine utility-scale solar generation with a 600 MWh battery energy storage system designed to shift daytime generation into peak demand periods after sunset.

Commercial structure

  • Generation: 1.2 GW solar PV capacity.
  • Storage: 600 MWh battery energy storage system.
  • Total debt package: up to US$566 million.
  • EBRD-arranged financing: up to US$265 million, including its own lending, a Bank of Jordan B-loan and concessional Clean Technology Fund financing.
  • Offtake: electricity is to be sold to the Egyptian Electricity Transmission Company under a 25-year power purchase agreement.

The EBRD says the project is expected to supply power equivalent to more than 1.4 million homes and avoid over 1.5 million tonnes of CO₂ emissions annually. Those are project expectations, not operating results.

Why it matters

This is a material project-finance milestone because it brings a very large generation asset and utility-scale storage into a defined debt structure backed by a long-term power purchase agreement. The battery component is strategically important: it is intended to make solar output more useful during evening peaks and support grid reliability, rather than relying on generation capacity alone.

For Egypt's power market, the transaction demonstrates continued mobilisation of private capital and development-finance institutions into large-scale renewable generation. For APL readers, the next test is execution: financing drawdown, construction and supply contracts, grid integration, and delivery against the planned capacity.

Next proof points

  • Confirmation of financing effectiveness, drawdown and any remaining conditions.
  • Disclosure of EPC, equipment-supply and battery-system contracts.
  • Visible construction progress and the timetable for grid connection and commercial operation.
  • Evidence that the storage system performs as intended under the project's offtake and dispatch arrangements.
