The institution

Project information made commercially meaningful.

African Project Ledger was established to close a persistent information gap: major projects are announced every day, but the commercial meaning behind those announcements is often fragmented across government releases, financing documents, contractor disclosures, procurement notices and market reporting.

APL brings those signals together and interprets what they may activate—capital deployment, procurement, partnerships, supply chains, construction capability, executive talent and the next milestones that determine whether a project becomes real.

We do not measure importance by the size of an announcement. We look for evidence of movement.
The APL difference

From headline to activation.

APL’s proprietary Project Activation Ledger follows projects through eight evidenced stages: Signal, Commitment, Financing, Procurement, Contract Award, Mobilisation, Delivery and Operation.

Each flagship dossier answers the questions decision-makers actually need:

  • What happened—and what has been verified?
  • Why does it matter commercially?
  • Who is positioned to participate?
  • Where is capital committed and where is the gap?
  • Which delivery frictions remain?
  • What must happen next for the project to remain credible?

Editorial mandate

Following Africa’s project economy.

APL covers infrastructure, transport and logistics, energy and utilities, construction and EPC, mining and industry, real estate and digital infrastructure. Coverage is organised around projects, capital flows, institutions, contractors and the commercial ecosystems forming around them.

Our editorial objective is not information volume. It is concise, sourced and decision-useful intelligence with a clear distinction between verified fact, analysis and matters still being monitored.

Who APL serves

Leaders deploying capital and capability.

APL is designed for investors, developers, EPC contractors, engineering firms, financial institutions, development-finance organisations, government bodies, professional advisers, project owners and executives operating across African markets.

Leadership

Roziana Ramjaun

Managing Partner & Publisher

Roziana Ramjaun leads African Project Ledger’s editorial direction, strategic positioning and institutional development.

An international executive and strategic adviser with more than 20 years of leadership experience across the United States, the United Kingdom and Mauritius, she has worked across business operations, property development, strategic communications, brand development, stakeholder engagement and executive leadership.

Her career includes management experience with Marriott International in the United States and senior executive responsibility within a Mauritian property-development and hospitality group. Her work has involved investors, property owners, contractors, architects, government stakeholders, corporate partners and multidisciplinary operational teams.

Through APL, Roziana brings an executive and commercially focused perspective to the projects shaping Africa. She oversees the publication’s approach to project intelligence—connecting major developments to financing, procurement, delivery capability, commercial opportunity and the organisations positioned to participate.

Her international background and cross-sector experience inform APL’s central purpose: translating fragmented project information into clear, credible and decision-useful intelligence for leaders operating across African markets.

Independence & accountability

Credibility is the operating model.

APL’s editorial conclusions are not determined by memberships, partnerships, recruitment mandates or sponsored engagements. Commercial relationships are separated from independent editorial judgment, and material errors are corrected transparently.
