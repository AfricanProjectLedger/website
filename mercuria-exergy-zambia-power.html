Mercuria and Exergy sign $250m Zambia power investment deal

The signed financing agreement targets electricity generation and transmission projects in Zambia, including infrastructure intended to support regional power trade. Funding remains subject to regulatory approvals.

Executive brief

Mercuria and Exergy announced a US$250 million financing deal in Lusaka on 25 September 2026. According to Mercuria, the capital is intended for projects developed by Exergy subsidiaries Lunzua Power Company and Lusitu Transmission and Distribution Company. Mercuria says several projects in the pipeline are already underway, but its release does not specify individual project allocations, capacities or disbursement dates.

Exergy combines generation, transmission and power trading businesses. Its development plans include a transmission link from Zambia toward the East African power market, alongside projects serving Zambia and the wider Southern and Eastern African region.

Why this is APL-worthy

A major global energy trader is committing private capital to both power generation and grid infrastructure in a market where reliable electricity is central to mining and industrial growth. The agreement also tests Zambia’s emerging open-access power market and its potential role as a regional electricity-trading hub.

What to watch

Regulatory approvals, named project allocations, financial drawdowns, transmission access arrangements and construction milestones will show how the signed commitment converts into operational assets.
