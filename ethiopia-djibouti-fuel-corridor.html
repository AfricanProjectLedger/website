Ethiopia–Djibouti launch $660m cross-border fuel corridor

Ethiopia, Djibouti and Dangote Group have launched a 120-kilometre refined-products pipeline and storage system designed to reshape fuel logistics along one of East Africa’s most important trade corridors.

Executive brief

The project will connect Damerjog in Djibouti with Dewele in Ethiopia through a multi-product pipeline, supported by more than one million cubic metres of combined storage capacity at the two ends. Official reporting places the investment at approximately $660 million and identifies Ethiopian Investment Holdings and Dangote Group as the development partners.

The system is intended to move imported petroleum products inland faster and with less reliance on long-distance road haulage. Ethiopia says the corridor could reduce the fuel journey from roughly five days to one, strengthen strategic reserves and connect more efficiently with rail, road and logistics infrastructure serving its domestic market.

Why this is APL-worthy

This is a sovereign-backed, cross-border energy and logistics platform combining pipeline, storage and multimodal distribution infrastructure. Its commercial significance extends beyond fuel transport: it could strengthen Djibouti’s gateway role, lower logistics friction for landlocked Ethiopia and create a substantial delivery pipeline across engineering, tanks, terminals, pumping systems, controls and corridor services.

What to watch

The launch now needs to convert into a defined project company, confirmed capital structure, permits, EPC awards and physical mobilisation. Those milestones will determine whether the announced two-year delivery ambition becomes bankable execution.
