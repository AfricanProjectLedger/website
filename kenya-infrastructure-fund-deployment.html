Kenya’s $2.62bn infrastructure fund begins deploying capital

Kenya’s National Infrastructure Fund has begun investing its KSh340 billion seed capital, marking the first disclosed execution step for a state-backed vehicle designed to mobilise long-term infrastructure finance.

Executive brief

The fund has started purchasing domestic government bonds and expects to deploy its full KSh340 billion capital base by June 2027, according to its chief executive, James Mworia. The portfolio is projected to generate about KSh42 billion annually while providing liquidity to the domestic banking system.

The vehicle targets KSh3.6 trillion in capital over the next decade through co-investment and debt. Its mandate covers commercially viable projects in energy, transport and logistics, information and communications technology, water and agriculture.

The initial capital was sourced from the monetisation of state assets, including Kenya Pipeline Company and part of the government’s Safaricom shareholding. The fund is assessing projects, including a possible investment in the proposed Dangote refinery in Lamu, but no project-level allocation was disclosed in the latest announcement.

Why this is APL-worthy

This moves Kenya’s infrastructure fund from legislation and institutional setup into active capital deployment. Its scale and proposed co-investment model could establish a new route for financing national infrastructure without relying solely on direct sovereign borrowing.

What to watch

The decisive test will be conversion of the bond portfolio into transparent project-level commitments. Watch for named investments, co-investor mandates, governance disclosures, procurement structures and the first financial closes across the fund’s priority sectors.
