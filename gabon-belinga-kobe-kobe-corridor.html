Gabon advances US$10bn Belinga–Kobe-Kobe infrastructure corridor

New agreements signed during Gabon’s November state visit to China bring engineering and logistics partners into a proposed mine-to-port system built around the Belinga iron ore deposit. The ambition is significant; the full financing package and delivery timetable are not yet publicly confirmed.

Executive brief

Gabon is advancing an integrated development intended to connect the Belinga iron ore deposit in Ogooué-Ivindo province with an Atlantic export terminal at Kobe-Kobe. Agreements signed in Shanghai on 5 November 2026 cover railway infrastructure, rolling stock and energy installations. Bloomberg reported that China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC) had been selected to construct the wider infrastructure corridor; the Gabonese government has separately confirmed the new partnership agreements.

The planned system combines the mine, a dedicated railway of approximately 535–550 kilometres, a mineral port at Kobe-Kobe and supporting power infrastructure. The port project covers a proposed 500-hectare site and includes four berths. A 400 MW hydroelectric installation at Booué has been identified as a potential energy source for the wider development. The overall infrastructure investment requirement has been reported at approximately US$10 billion; this is an estimated programme scale, not a disclosed amount of financing already secured.

Who is involved

The project brings together a multi-country group of mining, engineering, equipment, logistics and commercial partners. Gabonese authorities have identified Australia’s Fortescue as the mining partner through its local venture; CHEC as the railway and port construction partner; Gabon Power Company and China’s Sinohydro in the energy component; and US rail-equipment companies Wabtec and Progress Rail in the supply of wagons and locomotives. Africa Global Logistics (AGL) is involved in the port and railway arrangements and is expected to operate the Kobe-Kobe port. Swiss-based Trafigura is expected to support iron ore marketing. ALGEST Investment Bank is associated with the financial-structuring work.

These roles describe the announced project framework and should not be read as evidence that every construction, supply or operating contract has reached financial effectiveness or notice to proceed.

Why it matters

Belinga is not simply a mine-development story. Its commercial viability depends on synchronising extraction with a long-haul railway, a high-capacity export port and reliable energy supply. If delivered as planned, the corridor could create demand across civil engineering, rail systems, rolling stock, port equipment, power generation, logistics, mining services and local industrial supply chains.

For Gabon, the project is also positioned as an economic-diversification initiative: connecting resource development to domestic skills, local participation and potential mineral processing. The government has highlighted training and national workforce development as part of the project’s wider ambition. The scale of those economic benefits will depend on procurement terms, local-content execution, environmental and social safeguards, and the extent of value added inside Gabon.

What to watch

  • Financing: publication of a bankable financing plan, committed capital amounts, lender identities and the allocation of construction and market risks.
  • Delivery readiness: final engineering, route approvals, land access, environmental and social assessments, procurement packages and firm construction schedules for the railway and port.
  • Mine-to-port sequencing: evidence that mine development, rail capacity, port readiness and power supply are being advanced on a coordinated timetable.
  • Commercial contracts: confirmation of binding EPC, equipment, operating, offtake and logistics agreements, including their scope and conditions.
  • Local economic value: measurable commitments for Gabonese suppliers, workforce training, jobs, skills transfer and local processing.

APL assessment

Signal strength: high. Delivery certainty: still developing. The November agreements materially strengthen the partner architecture around Belinga, while the World Bank’s November discussions with Gabonese officials show that the corridor is being presented within the country’s wider infrastructure and development priorities. Neither development, on its own, establishes that the estimated US$10 billion has been fully financed. The next decisive proof point is a transparent financing and implementation structure followed by binding procurement and construction milestones.
